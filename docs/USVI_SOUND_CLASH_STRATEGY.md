## Executive Summary

**Sound Clash is not a cultural event waiting for a commemorative opportunity.** It's **durable educational infrastructure** that integrates federal ed-tech funding, corporate experience partnerships, and vocational training credentialing—validated by the SCID architecture model (see SCID_ARCHITECTURE.md).

**Core Thesis:**
The "homegrown technologist" pipeline isn't a diversity initiative. It's a **technical capacity-building model** where:
- Movement documentation generates AI training datasets (Digital Promise funding)
- Vocational training issues portable credentials (SCID/DACUM + ATProto VCs)
- Corporate partners gain authentic cultural engagement (not extraction)
- Federal infrastructure funding supports long-term sustainability (not one-time grants)

**Strategic Reframe:** Sound Clash operates at the **intersection of five funding ecosystems** that don't normally coordinate:
1. **Federal ed-tech infrastructure** (Digital Promise, IMLS, NSF)
2. **Corporate experiential marketing** (Amex, Verizon)
3. **Vocational training credentialing** (SCID competency schemas)
4. **Cultural sovereignty protocols** (CARE Principles, Indigenous data governance)
5. **Hospitality/tourism infrastructure** (Sonesta partnerships, place-based activation)

This intersection is the **organizational species model** (Strategic Framework §Core Thesis) in action.

### Layer 4: Cultural Sovereignty Protocols (CARE Principles)

**CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance:**
- **Collective Benefit:** Data supports community priorities (not extractive research)
- **Authority to Control:** Community determines data use, storage, sharing
- **Responsibility:** Data stewards accountable to cultural protocols
- **Ethics:** Data practices respect cultural values, minimize harm

**Sound Clash Application:**
- **Movement documentation governed by CARE:** Performers control how their movement data is used (training AI models, academic research, commercial licensing)
- **ATProto as sovereignty infrastructure:** DIDs owned by performers, not platform; verifiable credentials prove consent
- **SCID competency: Cultural Data Steward:** Homegrown technologists trained to operationalize CARE Principles (not just understand them theoretically)

**Why This Matters for Federal Funding:**
- **Digital Promise AI priority:** Culturally-grounded datasets that don't exploit source communities
- **IMLS civic memory mandate:** Accurate historical record requires consent-based archiving
- **NSF ethical AI research:** CARE-compliant data pipelines establish precedent for national policy

**Competitive Advantage:**
Most cultural datasets are extractive (researchers take, communities don't benefit). Sound Clash is **generative** (dataset creation *is* the workforce development model).

## IV. SCID Integration (Competency-Based Model)

From SCID_ARCHITECTURE.md, Sound Clash operationalizes three core competencies:

### Competency 1: Movement Documentation Technologist

**DACUM Duties:**
1. Video capture (mobile field recording during performance)
2. Sensor placement (motion capture, audio sync)
3. ATProto record creation (metadata tagging, consent logging)
4. Dataset quality assurance (validate completeness, check for corruption)

**Scaffolded Learning (PBL Phases):**
- **Phase 1 (Guided):** AI suggests camera angles, sensor positions; learner validates
- **Phase 2 (Assisted):** Learner operates equipment independently, AI helps troubleshoot
- **Phase 3 (Autonomous):** Learner manages full documentation pipeline, AI reference only

- Video files (Backblaze B2)
- Sensor data (motion capture logs)
- ATProto consent records (performer DIDs, timestamp)

## IX. Risks & Mitigations

### Risk 1: USVI Partner Reluctance (Institutional Buy-In)

**Risk:** USVI Department of Education, cultural institutions hesitant to co-apply (bureaucracy, unfamiliar model).

**Mitigation:**
- **Lead with Verizon VIL value:** $25K+/year + devices is concrete, immediate benefit (not abstract workforce development)
- **Show Digital Promise precedent:** Congressional charter org, $26M program, not experimental
- **Start with 1-2 pilot schools:** Not territory-wide rollout; reduces institutional friction

---

### Risk 2: Digital Promise Rejects LOI (Not Competitive)

**Risk:** Digital Promise receives many applications; Sound Clash may not be selected.

**Mitigation:**
- **CARE Principles differentiation:** Most AI dataset proposals don't address Indigenous/diaspora data sovereignty
- **Verizon VIL integration:** If USVI schools already in VIL, Digital Promise sees Sound Clash as extending existing investment
- **Multiple funding layers:** Even without Digital Promise, Amex + Verizon + IMLS still viable

---

### Risk 3: Amex Sponsorship Terms Unfavorable (Revenue Share Too High)

**Risk:** Amex demands 30-50% merchandise revenue (vs. projected 10-20%), reducing earned income.

**Mitigation:**
- **Negotiate upfront sponsorship fee:** Higher annual fee ($100K-150K) in exchange for lower revenue share (10-15%)
- **Co-branding value:** Amex gets brand association with authentic cultural programming (not extractive festival); this is worth premium to them
- **Walk-away threshold:** If Amex terms exceed 25% revenue share, pursue alternative sponsor (Mastercard, Visa have similar experiential programs)

---

### Risk 4: SCID Schema Overhead (Too Complex for First Cohort)

**Risk:** Building 3 competency schemas + mobile UI + VC infrastructure before first cohort delays deployment.

**Mitigation:**
- **Start with 1 competency:** Movement Documentation Technologist (highest Digital Promise alignment)
- **Manual VC issuance Year 1:** Use ATProto CLI tools, not automated assessment logic (reduces dev cost)
- **Scale incrementally:** Add Cultural Data Steward (Year 2), Experiential Production (Year 3) as infrastructure matures
