## Title

**The Kitchen as Stage**

---

## Abstract

Dining is already theatrical. Entry, pacing, reveal, and departure are staged whether or not anyone names them that way. This poster presents a simple grammar for treating a dining or kitchen venue as a theatrical room: one shared structure with four facets — how the space sounds, what the sequence of the meal means, how the room is run, and what it costs — governed by a different party at each render (venue, guest, operator, vendor), with all four present at once but only one governing at a time.

The poster walks through this grammar as applied to a working hospitality project built around wine tasting and chef-led story formats. A facilitator sits at the center of each session, bound by a stated performance constraint: never center themselves, always orbit and defer to the person whose knowledge is being shared — enforced the way a director enforces blocking, not offered as a courtesy. Gestures made during a tasting — how a hand presses, how a texture is demonstrated — are captured, timestamped, and named as stops in the session, the same discipline a stage manager applies to a performer's physical marks. A small, fixed set of narrative forms — two-voice dialogue, single-speaker address, brief announcement — each carries its own assigned voice and function across every session, the way a company assigns a role to an actor rather than improvising casting each night.

Hospitality and live events already borrow theatrical vocabulary informally — front of house, the reveal, staging a plate. This work tests whether that borrowing can be made rigorous: a facilitator's role, a guest's gesture, and a session's narrative form treated as a cast, a blocking chart, and a script, rather than separate concerns bolted on after the fact. For designers and technologists who already treat the venue as the unit of design, this offers a transferable structure for spaces built around food and hospitality — domains facing the same staging problems as the stage, rarely described in the same language.
