## Title

**The Kitchen as Stage**

---

## Abstract

Dining is already theatrical. Entry, pacing, reveal, and departure are staged whether or not anyone names them that way. This poster presents a simple grammar for treating a dining or kitchen venue as a theatrical room: one shared structure with four facets — how the space sounds, what the sequence of the meal means, how the room is run, and what it costs — governed by a different party at each render (venue, guest, operator, vendor), with all four present at once but only one governing at a time.

The poster walks through this grammar as applied to a working hospitality project built around wine tasting and chef-told story formats: a venue acoustic model keyed to the room's physical geometry, feeding real-time spatial audio; a narrative "chapter" structure governing what a guest may carry away from the experience and under what consent; and an operational layer treating the room itself as a managed unit, the way a stage manager treats a set.

Hospitality and live events already borrow theatrical vocabulary informally — front of house, the reveal, staging a plate. This work tests whether that borrowing can be made rigorous: one shared model where acoustics, narrative sequencing, operations, and commerce are explicit, co-occurring facets of a single room rather than separate concerns bolted on after the fact. For designers and technologists who already treat the venue as the unit of design, this offers a transferable structure for spaces built around food and hospitality — domains facing the same staging problems as the stage, rarely described in the same language.
