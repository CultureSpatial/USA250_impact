## Why this document exists

The Canada work to date has two layers, and they need different treatment:

1. **Record hygiene** — are the RFQ IDs current, is the BC Bid profile live, has registration lapsed. Tracked in PART-135 / PART-136. Necessary, but backward-looking.
2. **Strategic validity** — does the *world* those records assumed still exist. Not tracked anywhere. This document.

Auditing solicitation IDs while the underlying market thesis has shifted is precision applied to the wrong question. The findings below are the wider lens: what changed, which notions we should stop carrying, and where the actual opportunity now sits.

**Four load-bearing assumptions in the BC thread are no longer valid.** One of them opens a stronger opportunity than the one originally planned.

## Part 2 — Notions no longer valid

### 1. FIFA World Cup 2026 has concluded

The tournament ran **June 11 – July 19, 2026**. It is over. Destination Vancouver is publicly in past tense — "demonstrated Vancouver's ability to deliver one of the world's largest sporting events" — and has pivoted to lasting-impact framing, targeting growth of the visitor economy from **$9B to $11B** and citing **$1B+ in tourism economic impact across 2026–2031**.

A large share of the workspace is written as *pre*-FIFA activation: OPS-57 (FIFA Wine Corridor), PART-102 (Fan Camp), PART-121 (Fan Zone catering), OPS-98 (7 Vancouver match windows), OPS-176 (venue kiosk brief), PART-63/64 (Lyrical Host), PART-70/71, PART-103, RES-58. **These are now retrospective.** Any that still read as "activate before the tournament" are dead as written.

**This is not purely a loss.** The legacy window is open and DMOs are actively courting it — Destination Vancouver is explicitly seeking what compounds the event into 2031. OPS-84 ("SeattleFWC26 legacy", In Progress) is already the correct posture. But legacy windows close fast: institutional attention on "what did we learn" runs roughly two to three quarters post-event. **That clock is already ~1 month in.**

### 2. The cross-border consumer wine corridor is politically radioactive

This is the sharpest reversal, and it strikes directly at OPS-96 and OPS-57.

- Since **March 2025**, American alcohol has been pulled from liquor store shelves across nearly all of Canada in retaliation for US tariffs
- **BC has held firm.** Premier David Eby: *"There is not a chance in hell that U.S. alcohol is going back on the shelf."*
- **July 2026:** the US imposed **50% tariffs** on selected Canadian imports, explicitly citing provincial discrimination against American alcohol
- BC and Ontario **refused to fold**; only Alberta and Saskatchewan lifted restrictions (June 2025)
- Canadian imports of US alcohol fell **~81%** — roughly US$718M → US$137M

A Seattle↔Vancouver wine corridor pitched as consumer-facing cultural exchange is, in this environment, asking two governments in an active trade dispute to co-sponsor the exact category they are fighting over. **That framing should be retired.**

### 3. …but a *production* corridor is live, in the opposite direction

Here the research turns up something genuinely useful.

BC wineries have been **importing Washington grapes and juice since 2024** under the Vintage Replacement Program, extended through the 2025 production year and sustaining supply into 2026. Most wineries "looked directly south to Washington state for replacement grapes," with additional sourcing from Oregon, California, Ontario and New Zealand.

**The BC–WA wine corridor exists. It is upstream (bulk grape and juice supply), not downstream (retail and consumer experience).** It is real, active, economically material, and politically uncontroversial — because it is trade *into* BC, not US product on BC shelves.

OPS-96's proof-transfer thesis is not dead. It is **pointed at the wrong axis**. The corridor to document, instrument and govern is the production/supply one.

### 4. BC wine is in contraction, and the buyer is distressed

The **January 2024 polar vortex** caused roughly **95% wine grape crop loss** and **~20% vine death**, with temperatures below −20°C for several days in the Okanagan-Similkameen.

Current state:

- The province remains **10,000+ tonnes short** of the grape volume required to meet market demand (WGBC and BC Grapegrowers' Association estimate)
- Replanted vines **will not yield usable fruit for three to four years** — recovery runs into **2027–2028**
- In 2024 **at least a quarter of Okanagan wineries were listed for sale**; roughly 20 publicly on the market; multiple wineries now in **foreclosure**
- Sector scale remains significant: **$3.75B/yr** to the provincial economy, **14,000 FTE**, **1.2M wine tourism visitors**, **$476M** in tourism-related impact

**The implication for our pricing is direct.** `B2B_VOCATIONAL_TRAINING.md` models Foundation Cohort at $75K, Infrastructure Co-Design at $150K, Continuous Capacity at $200K/yr. A sector where a quarter of operators are for sale and several are in foreclosure does not buy $75K discretionary cultural-tech pilots. **Individual wineries are not the buyer here. Institutions holding recovery money are.**

## Part 3 — The opportunity that got stronger

### Provenance and truth-in-labelling — the strongest reframe available

The Vintage Replacement Program created a **two-tier labelling regime**: wines made from BC fruit versus wines "crafted in BC" from imported grapes and juice. WGBC is working with the **BC Grapegrowers' Association, the Liquor Distribution Branch and CFIA** to ensure wineries have what they need on **truth-in-labelling policy**, while "laying the groundwork for a coordinated industry plan for **2026**."

Read that as a systems problem and it is exactly ours:

- **Provenance tracking** — which fruit, from where, in which bottling
- **Verifiable attribution** — claims a regulator and a consumer can both check
- **Disclosure without stigma** — "crafted in BC" needs to be communicated as legitimate, not as a confession
- **Multi-party governance** — producers, association, Crown distributor, federal regulator, each with different authority over the same record

Our stack already targets this: ATProto provenance records, story-signing under exit-time licensing, the Cultural Stack layering in PART-61, CARE/OCAP governance. **The pitch stops being "cultural experience layer for wine tourism" — a nice-to-have to a distressed sector — and becomes "provenance infrastructure for a regulatory disclosure obligation."** That is a need with government and association urgency behind it, on a 2026 planning timeline, with named institutional owners.

**This deserves validation as the primary Canada thesis.** It is not a repositioning of the same offer; it is a different buyer, budget line, and urgency class.

### Secondary opportunities

**Replant and climate-adaptation data.** The **Enhanced Replant Program** (launched Nov 2024, second intake fall 2025, administered by the Investment Agriculture Foundation) funds replanting with climate-resilient varieties. PART-60's "Watershed-to-Wine Climate Adaptation Dashboard" thesis is **well-aimed at this** — it is simply pointed at a Destination BC tourism-marketing SOA rather than at agricultural resilience money. **Right idea, wrong envelope.**

**Indigenous Tourism BC / sovereign storytelling (PART-58).** Least exposed to the wine sector's distress, aligned with CARE/OCAP work we have already done, and a named BC Tourism FC consortium member. PART-57 correctly identifies it as the strongest bridge. Its lapsed date (see PART-135) is the most urgent single item in the Canada thread.

**Practitioner continuity through consolidation.** Winery sales and foreclosures displace skilled workers. Competency capture and portable credentialing (SCID/DACUM, `SCID_ARCHITECTURE.md`) address a live workforce problem rather than a speculative one — and it is the version of the WGBC "practitioner participation governance layer" (PART-25) that survives contact with 2026.

**FIFA legacy window.** Time-boxed and closing. Whatever we can evidence about what happened across the Seattle–Vancouver corridor during the tournament has more institutional value now than it will in six months.

## Part 4 — Risk register

| Risk | Severity | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade war escalation** | High | Active and escalating as of July 2026. Any cross-border wine framing carries political exposure we do not control. Mitigate by pitching production-side and non-alcohol cultural content, not consumer wine exchange. |
| **Customer insolvency** | High | A quarter of Okanagan wineries listed for sale; foreclosures underway. Sell to institutions holding recovery money, not to individual distressed operators. |
| **Recovery-timeline mismatch** | Medium-High | Vines don't yield until 2027–2028. A thesis dependent on a recovered BC wine sector is a 2028 thesis. Provenance/labelling work is needed *during* the gap, which is why it is the stronger play. |
| **FIFA legacy window closing** | Medium | Institutional attention on legacy runs ~2–3 quarters post-event. Already ~1 month elapsed. |

## Part 5 — Notions to stop carrying

Explicit kill list. These should not appear in any new Canada-facing material:

1. **"Pre-FIFA activation"** — the tournament is over. Legacy framing only.
2. **"Seattle–Vancouver consumer wine corridor"** — politically radioactive. Reframe to production-side, or to non-alcohol cultural/culinary content.
3. **"BC Wine Institute / BCWI"** — the organization is Wine Growers British Columbia.
4. **"Expanding interactive wine experiences"** (PART-57 cites BC Wine Tourism Strategy 2023–2027 on this) — that strategy predates the freeze. The sector's stated priority is survival and supply, not experience expansion.
5. **Winery-as-buyer at $75K+** — wrong buyer, wrong number, wrong year.
6. **VanWineFest as the validation venue** (PART-50, PART-47) — verify the festival's own post-2024 state before treating it as a proof surface.
7. **The 2027–2028 due dates as deadlines** — almost certainly SOA term-ends (PART-135).

---

## Part 6 — Re-pointing recommendations

1. **Validate the provenance/labelling thesis first.** One conversation with WGBC about truth-in-labelling for the 2026 industry plan is worth more than perfecting a supplier profile for a possibly-closed SOA. This is the highest-value next action in the entire Canada thread.
2. **Resolve PART-58 (RFI26DBC68) immediately** — lapsed date, strongest bridge, least sector-distress exposure.
3. **Re-point OPS-96** from consumer corridor to production corridor, or to non-wine cultural content. The proof-transfer logic is sound; the axis is wrong.
4. **Re-point PART-60** from a Destination BC tourism SOA toward agricultural/climate-resilience funding (Enhanced Replant Program, Investment Agriculture Foundation, PacifiCan).
5. **Reconcile the PART-25 sub-series** — two duplicate A/B/C chains with divergent statuses; collapse to one under WGBC naming.
6. **Convert FIFA issues to legacy framing or close them.** Do it now, while the window is open.
7. **Re-baseline BC pricing** against institutional buyers, not winery operators.

---

## Standing practice this establishes

The failure mode this document corrects is not "stale IDs." It is **carrying a market thesis forward without re-testing its premises** — the same pattern as NSF SCiPE (pursued after closure and after the institutional fit proved wrong), and the same pattern the strategy documents' v1.0→v2.0 revision addressed.

**Proposed rule:** any funding or partnership thread that has been open more than two quarters gets a premise re-test, not just a status check. The question is not "is this record current" but "is the world this record assumed still there."
